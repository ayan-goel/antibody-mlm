## How these are computed

- **Baseline:** `uniform_medium` (random masking), now itself a 3-seed mean.
- **Metrics — two sets, deliberately.** Table 1 reports **all seven** metrics from the paper's main table. Table 2 collapses them to **five task-level axes** (one metric per functional axis) so that no axis is double-counted when taking a median or counting wins — paratope and contact each contribute two of the seven. The mapping: CDR3 = `infill_cdr3_exact_match`, Para = `ds_paratope_auprc_mean`, Cont = `ds_contact_map_long_range_precision_at_L_mean`, Struc = `ds_structure_probe_spearman_distance_mean`, Dev = `ds_developability_spearman_macro_mean`. The two dropped from the summary (`P. MCC`, `C. AUROC`) have **zero resolved effects for any strategy**, so nothing is lost.
- **Contrasts are PAIRED within seed** — compute `strategy_s − uniform_s` for each seed, then average. All seeds share an identical held-out split and evaluation sample, so the paired difference is far tighter than differencing the marginals. Quote these, not differences of Table 1 means.
- **Resolved (`*`) means |mean/sd| ≥ 2** across the three seeds. Anything below that is **unresolved at n=3** — not "no effect". With n=3 the sd estimate is itself unstable; never write "no effect".

### The random-mixture control (answers #5.5, #1.2, #2)

`hybrid-RANDOM` holds everything identical to `hybrid` — same six sub-strategies, same 6,250-step block structure, same budget — and replaces only the *values*: at each block the mixture is redrawn uniformly from the simplex, Dirichlet(1,…,1). The seed drives the draw, so the three replicates sample three **different** arbitrary curricula. Config: `configs/hybrid_random_medium.yaml`; implementation is `random_policy` in `masking/hybrid.py`.

Paired against the control, **no designed curriculum resolves on any metric**:

| vs `hybrid-RANDOM` | CDR3 | P. AUPRC | P. MCC | C. AUROC | C. P@L | Str. ρ | Dev. ρ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| hybrid | −0.003 (−0.3σ) | −0.019 (−0.8σ) | −0.026 (−1.5σ) | +0.004 (+0.6σ) | +0.027 (+0.5σ) | +0.008 (+0.8σ) | −0.003 (−0.0σ) |
| hybrid-stretched | −0.005 (−0.3σ) | +0.000 (+0.0σ) | −0.001 (−0.0σ) | +0.008 (+1.3σ) | +0.065 (+1.3σ) | +0.015 (+0.7σ) | +0.012 (+0.1σ) |

Both recover the same CDR3 gain over uniform that the designed schedules do (control +0.162 at 8.2σ; `hybrid` +0.159 at 12.0σ). **Mixing specialists at all is what produces the effect; which mixture, and the schedule over it, is not load-bearing.** That is the direct answer to "the experiments do not isolate which components matter" and "is performance sensitive to the choice of values?".

**`hybrid-adaptive` is worth surfacing — it answers reviewer #1.3 directly.** They asked whether masking weights could be assigned *dynamically from predictive performance* rather than pre-set. That is exactly what this is: it tracks an EMA of per-strategy training loss (decay 0.99, ~70-batch half-life) and softmaxes it (temperature 0.5) into the sampling distribution, so sub-strategies the model is currently worst at receive more mask budget, multiplied against a static base policy so nothing starves. Config: `configs/hybrid_adaptive_medium.yaml`, implemented via `adaptive=True` in `masking/hybrid.py`.

On seed 42 it is the best hybrid on CDR3 (0.203) and P. MCC (0.648) and the worst on Dev. ρ (0.232 — though that run had 1/5 developability probe seeds collapse, so discount it). **We can tell #1.3 we implemented and trained their suggestion.** Caveat to state: it is the only variant that is genuinely non-deterministic — the loss-feedback loop depends on noisy per-batch losses, so the same seed does not reproduce the same curriculum. Replicating it needs more seeds than the fixed-schedule hybrids, not fewer.

**`hybrid-intersection`** adds the `intersection` masker as a seventh sub-strategy. Relevant because `intersection` turned out to be the strongest specialist (3/5 resolved wins, best paratope). It leads the hybrids on P. AUPRC (0.845) but is worst on C. P@L (0.572).

**`hybrid-paired` and `multispecific` are not comparable and were never evaluated.** Both train on `oas_paired_500k.jsonl` with `paired: true` — a different corpus and tokenization regime — so VH-only benchmark numbers would not be apples-to-apples. `scripts/generate_report.py` excludes them by design (`PAIRED_EXPERIMENTS`). They appear in `comparison_table.csv` with training metrics only; all seven downstream columns are empty. Evaluating them would take a separate paired-chain benchmark suite, which does not exist here.

## Interpretation

- **The CDR3 effect is the strongest result in the work** (24σ, 14σ) — but see the caveat below on *what* is being recovered.
- **`interface`'s paratope gain is the most reproducible non-CDR3 result**: +0.042 at **19.2σ**, per-seed +0.044/+0.043/+0.040.
- **Long-range contact gains survive for `interface` (2.3σ) and `structure-LR` (2.6σ)** — but not for plain `structure`, which buys nothing and costs paratope and CDR3.
- **`intersection` is underrated** by the paper: 3/5 resolved wins, best Para of any strategy.
- **Resolved effects cluster in three metrics only** — CDR3, Para, Cont. **Zero** resolved effects on Struc, Dev, C.AUROC or P.MCC for *any* strategy. Those columns cannot support the differences the paper reports.
- **Sign consistency is worth reporting where individual contrasts don't resolve**: Dev is negative for 6 of 7 strategies, Struc positive for 6 of 7. Real small effects the design is underpowered to resolve.

## Caveats to remember

- **CDR3 recovery is consensus reproduction, not design.** Stratified by training-split frequency, exact match is **0.000 for every strategy on every CDR3 seen <10× in training** (265 of 1,000 eval sequences, incl. all 162 never seen). `cdr` scores 0.395 on CDR3s seen ≥100×. The completions are *not* degenerate strings (zero homopolymers, correct AR…/…DL motifs), but the best-scoring strategies are the least diverse (`cdr` distinct 0.371, novel 0.311 vs `uniform` 0.563 / 0.817). Report the stratified table.
- **The Dev column above is from the unstable SGD probe.** TAP is 169 train / 24 val / 48 test against a 2,560-parameter head; 7 runs had probe seeds collapse at epoch ≤8, two of them published. Neither a lower LR nor MSE-based selection fixes it — the task is data-starved. `scripts/run_developability_ridge.py` (closed-form ridge, nested 5-fold CV over all 241 antibodies) reduces seed variance ~5× and is the number to cite once it lands.
- **Report ≈5.9× or "4%→23% exact match"**, not "+493%", for the CDR3 claim — a percentage on a 0.039 base is not reviewer-proof.
- **Median, never mean** across tasks (CDR3's near-zero baseline distorts the mean).
- ~~Three hybrids are still single-seed~~ **Resolved 2026-08-15: every hybrid is n=3.** The only n=1 arm left is `interface-permuted`, the label-permutation control.
- **`hybrid_warmstart` has a compute confound**: 175k steps vs 125k, warm-started from `interface`. Not caught by any reviewer; match the compute or state it.
